## About this post

Personal experience from Omshree Butani, an AWS Community Builder who attended as a first-time All Builders Welcome grantee, published on AWS Builder Center on November 2, 2026. The venues it describes (including Mandalay Bay) match re:Invent 2025, not 2026. It is not official AWS guidance. Dates, venues, benefits and rules for re:Invent 2026 come from the official re:Invent pages and the ABW Terms and Conditions, which win if anything here differs.

## Venues and timing

- Sessions are spread over several Strip hotels linked by shuttles. The author suggests 30–45 minutes between sessions at different venues and 15–20 minutes within the same venue.
- Pick one or two home-base venues per day and use the AWS Events app.

## Keynotes and sessions

- Go to one keynote in person and catch the rest on replay.
- Chalk talks are interactive and small (the author says roughly 30–50 people). Workshops fill fast. Builders' sessions are about an hour of guided building and you leave with working code.
- Reserve as soon as reservations open. If you miss out, arrive about 15 minutes early for a walk-up seat and check the app daily for newly added sessions.
- The post is inconsistent about which session types are recorded, so check the official session pages for recordings.
- Match the session level to what you already know, and go deeper in areas you know well.

## Planning the week

Build a skeleton schedule with four or five must-see items and keep two to three hours a day open for things you discover on site.

## Expo

Use it on purpose: ask engineers at AWS service booths real questions, talk to startups, find where AWS Heroes and Community Builders gather, and try demos. The author skipped badge-scan giveaways and long swag lines.

## Networking

- Aim for real conversations: ask people what they are working on.
- Go to grantee events and Birds of a Feather sessions, and use hallway time.
- Follow up on LinkedIn the same day. A few real connections matter more than a stack of business cards.
- re:Play is worth going to once; it is fine to leave after an hour. Smaller community dinners and meetups, announced on Slack, X and LinkedIn, often give better conversations.

## Looking after yourself

Wear broken-in shoes (the author walked 20,000+ steps a day), aim for about seven hours of sleep, leave parties early, carry snacks and water, and schedule breaks. Imposter syndrome is common; being selected means the organizers wanted you there, so ask questions and introduce yourself.

## Capturing what you learn

Write three bullet takeaways per session, photograph slides where allowed, record short voice memos, and note who you talked to. Start on day one.

## After the event

Stay involved through the AWS Community Builders program, user groups, AWS Summits, open source, and by writing or talking about what you learned.
